Kindling

1The problem

K-12 edtech companies grow by getting teachers to adopt for free, then converting at the school or district level when enough teachers are hooked. But knowing when to reach out is the hard part.

The two questions that drive every GTM motion in K-12 edtech:

PQL
Product Qualified Lead — a free school where enough teachers are using the product that someone in the building should be buying a license. When do you reach out?
Health
Churn Risk — a paying school where teacher activity has fallen off a cliff. When do you call your CS rep before renewal?

The naive approach: set a usage threshold (e.g. "5+ teachers built something this week") and fire whenever a school crosses it. The naive approach explodes every December.

The school calendar problem nobody talks about
Schools stop using everything over winter break — Thanksgiving, December, spring break. A naive model sees usage drop to zero and fires a churn alarm on every single paying school, every single year. When 100% of your alerts are false alarms, your CS team stops acting on them.

Kindling's answer: measure activity in school days, not calendar days.

2The core innovation — school-day windows

The single idea this project is built around: a "quiet week" means something completely different during winter break than it does in October.

Instead of asking "what happened in the last 7 calendar days?", Kindling asks: "what happened during the last 10 instructional days?"

School calendar (2025–26, US) ───────────────────────────────────────────────────── Breaks (instructional days paused): Thanksgiving Nov 26 – Nov 30 Winter Dec 22 – Jan 4 Spring Mar 23 – Mar 27 Summer Jun 13 – Aug 31 Calendar window (naive): School-day window (Kindling): ────────────────────────────── ────────────────────────────── Dec 15: 10 days = Dec 5–14 ✓ Dec 15: 10 instr. days = Dec 1–12 Dec 26: 10 days = Dec 16–25 ✗ Dec 26: 10 instr. days = Dec 1–12 ↑ break week included ↑ break skipped entirely Jan 5: 10 days = Dec 26–Jan 4 ✗ Jan 5: 10 instr. days = Dec 1–12 ↑ entire window is break ↑ still the pre-break window Result: naive model fires false alarms every break. Kindling never fires during break — the window doesn't move.
Why this is the right model for K-12
A school's usage patterns should be measured against the days they were actually supposed to be using it. A teacher who hasn't logged in for two weeks of winter break isn't churning — she's on vacation. The scoring engine should know that.

The school-day window is built in kindling/features.py using a DuckDB query that filters the event table to instructional days only before computing any usage statistics.

3The pipeline, end to end

Five layers. Each has a clear job.

synthetic event data (200 schools, 1,760 teachers, 347K events) │ ▼ features.py DuckDB SQL — computes per-school stats in school-day windows [deterministic] → teacher count, session trend, builder ratio, seat utilisation │ ▼ pql.py Rules scorer 0–100, top-3 signal reasons, PQL_READY_THRESHOLD ≥60 [deterministic] health.py Rules scorer 0–100, 4 signals, HEALTH_RISK_THRESHOLD ≤70 [deterministic] │ ▼ api.py FastAPI — serves scores to the React frontend and n8n [deterministic] GET /pql, GET /health, GET /stats, GET /prospects/top10, GET /pipeline/funnel … │ ▼ n8n cloud Three scheduled workflows — weekly brief, on-demand alert, [automation] follow-up reminders — all posting to Slack │ ▼ Claude Haiku Drafts the outreach email embedded in the Monday #sales brief [AI] → under 80 words, opens with a specific signal number, 3rd-grade reading level

Two outputs, two destinations: the Monday brief (ranked schools + AI email) goes to #sales; churn alerts go to #customer-success.

4How the scoring works

Both scorers are rules-based — not machine learning. That's intentional. A rule you can read and argue with is more trustworthy than a black box in a sales context.

PQL score (0–100) — free schools

Four signals, weighted and capped at 100:

SignalWeightWhat it measures
Teacher trend35 ptsAre the number of active teachers growing week over week?
Session trend30 ptsAre sessions (time spent) trending up?
Builder ratio20 ptsWhat fraction of teachers have built something (not just viewed)?
Multi-teacher15 ptsAre multiple teachers using it independently (not just one champion)?

Threshold: PQL_READY_THRESHOLD = 60 → scores ≥ 60 appear in the "Ready to Buy" list.

Each school also gets top-3 signal reasons in plain English (e.g. "3 teachers built projects last week, up from 1") — this is what goes into the Claude email prompt and the UI drawer.

Health score (0–100) — paying schools

SignalWeightWhat it measures
Teacher trend40 ptsAre active teachers trending up or down over the last 10 instr. days?
Session trend35 ptsTotal session depth — are teachers still going deep?
Renewal urgency15 ptsHow close is the renewal date? Flags schools within 30 days.
Seat utilisation10 ptsWhat % of licensed seats have ever been used?

Threshold: HEALTH_RISK_THRESHOLD = 70 → scores ≤ 70 appear in the "At Risk" list.

Prospect priority score — cold leads

For schools that haven't yet been touched (PQL = 0 or low, never contacted), a separate ranking decides who to reach out to first:

SignalWeight
Contact info on file40 pts
District-level school (higher contract value)25 pts
Paying customers in the same state (social proof)25 pts
Prior engagement (any activity at all)10 pts
Why rules, not ML?
A logistic regression was trained and tested (fall training set, spring test set). Results: LR gets higher recall on health (55% vs 27%) but an 88% false alarm rate — it still doesn't understand breaks. The rules model gets 0% false alarms because the breaks are hard-coded as domain knowledge. ML doesn't know what a school calendar is.

5Backtest results — the numbers

Both scorers are backtested by replaying every Monday from September 2025 to July 2026 (a full school year). At each Monday snapshot, the scorer only sees data up to that date — no future leakage. Then the actual conversion outcomes are looked up and the scorer is graded.

PQL backtest — Kindling vs naive

73%
Kindling precision (flagged schools that converted)
37%
Naive precision (5+ teachers built = ready)
30
Kindling: schools flagged total (vs 93 naive)
30 days
Kindling median lead time (vs 53 days naive)

Read: Kindling sends reps about a third as many schools, and twice as many of them actually buy. The trade-off is recall — Kindling catches 65% of converters (22/34) vs naive's 100%. The 6 it misses are "quiet converters" with only 2 teachers.

Health backtest — the proof the core idea works

0%
Kindling false alarm rate (school-day windows) — healthy schools never flagged at risk
100%
Naive false alarm rate (calendar windows) — every healthy school flagged at-risk over winter break
The school-day windows proof
On the January 5th snapshot, the naive calendar-window model had flagged 37 false alarms (every healthy school looked like it was churning — because all usage dropped over winter break). The school-day window model had 3. That's the whole idea, confirmed in real simulated data.

6The 3 n8n automations

All three workflows run on n8n cloud. n8n is a visual workflow automation tool — it handles scheduling, calling the API, talking to Slack, and the Anthropic API call, with no code required. The local FastAPI server is exposed to n8n via localtunnel (a temporary public URL that points at localhost:8000).

Monday 8:00 AM · kindling_weekly.json · "Kindling notify (manual)"

Weekly Monday brief

The main workflow. Calls GET /pql → picks schools with score ≥ 60 → calls GET /health → picks at-risk schools → calls Claude Haiku to draft a personalized cold email (under 80 words, opens with a specific usage number, addressed to the contact's first name) → posts two Slack messages: ranked ready-to-buy list with AI email + Gmail deeplink to #sales, and at-risk list to #customer-success.

On-demand webhook · kindling_notify.json · "Kindling notify (manual)"

Single-school alert

Triggered from the React GTM console when a rep clicks "Send to Slack" on any school. Routes to #sales for Ready schools, #customer-success for At Risk. Logs the outreach to data/outreach_log.json (timestamp, school, channel, PQL at time of contact) so the follow-up workflow knows who was contacted and when. After publishing, its production webhook URL is stored in .env as N8N_NOTIFY_WEBHOOK.

Monday 9:00 AM · kindling_followup.json · "Kindling follow-up reminders"

Stale lead follow-up

Runs one hour after the weekly brief. Calls GET /outreach/stale?days=14 — which returns schools that were contacted 14+ days ago with no PQL improvement since. If any are found, posts a nudge to #sales: "Still waiting on a reply from these schools — worth a follow-up?"

How the Claude email drafting works

The Anthropic API call is a node inside kindling_weekly. The API key is stored as an n8n Header Auth credential — it lives only in n8n's encrypted credential store, never in any file or environment variable.

The email prompt is engineered around SDR best practices:

  • Open with a specific signal number ("4 teachers at Lincoln built projects last week") — not generic
  • First paragraph never starts with "I"
  • Banned words: "noticed," "data," "metrics," "reaching out," "hope this finds you"
  • Under 80 words, 3rd-grade reading level, one question only, no links
  • Output format: SUBJECT: / BODY: on two lines (avoids JSON parsing complexity)
  • Passes contact name, title, and location so the email opens "Hi Sarah," not "Hi there,"

7The GTM console (React app)

A React web app at localhost:5173 gives the sales and CS team a live view of every school in the funnel. Seven screens:

Monday Brief

Landing screen. Four clickable KPI tiles (Ready to Buy, At Risk, Total ARR, NRR with inline breakdown). A watch list of 3 at-risk schools — always fills to 3, using real at-risk schools first and lowest-health schools flagged _watch if fewer. A prospects card showing the top 3 cold leads. Everything is clickable and navigates to the relevant screen.

Ready to Buy

Ranked list of PQL-ready free schools. Click any row to open a drawer with school details, signal breakdown, top-3 reasons, contact info, and a "Send to #sales" Slack button (routed via n8n).

At Risk

Paying schools with health score ≤ 70. Shows renewal date, ARR, and health signals. Drawer includes a "Send to #customer-success" button.

Prospects

Top 10 cold leads ranked by priority score. Expandable signal breakdown per school. Click a row → OutreachBar appears with four channels: Slack (via n8n → #sales), Gmail compose deeplink with pre-filled subject and body, LinkedIn contact search, Twitter/X school search. Cold email template for PQL=0 schools; dormant template for PQL 1–19.

Pipeline

Funnel view. School counts at each stage: Cold → Dormant → Active → Ready → Converted. Shows how many schools are at each step in the buying journey.

Backtest

Interactive comparison of Kindling vs naive model across a full school year. Bar chart showing precision, recall, and false alarm rate week by week. Break periods are shaded so the winter/spring effect is visually obvious.

Automations

n8n run history. Shows the last N workflow executions, their status, and timestamp — so the team can see when the last Monday brief fired.

9Honest limits

Synthetic data only
Every school, teacher, and event is generated by simulate.py. The simulator is carefully designed to stress-test the scorer (break seasonality, stalled schools, quiet converters), but real data will be messier. The scoring rules will need calibration once connected to production.
LimitWhat it means in production
One shared school calendarAll 200 schools use the same national break calendar. Real districts have different break dates — a Houston school has different holidays than a Boston school. Production needs per-district calendars.
localtunnel URL changes on restartEvery time the laptop reboots, the localtunnel URL changes and 4 HTTP Request nodes across 2 n8n workflows need manual updates. This is the most common operational pain point.
outreach_log.json is a flat fileFine for testing (one person using it). In production this belongs in a database with proper indexing on school ID and timestamp.
Stalled schools are unbeatableSchools that look like buyers but never buy (budget freeze, committee approval required) are indistinguishable from real buyers with this data alone. This is the honest precision ceiling — not a bug in the scorer.
No expansion revenue in NRRSeat counts and ARR are static in the simulator. Real NRR would include expansion dollars when a district adds more seats.

10Where it goes next

To connect real data

1
Replace kindling/simulate.py with a live query against the production database (or a read replica). The schema Kindling expects: school_id, name, city, state, contact_name, contact_title, contact_email, license_seats, renewal_date, arr.
2
Run the backtests on actual conversion history to calibrate thresholds: python -m kindling.backtest pql and python -m kindling.backtest health. Tune PQL_READY_THRESHOLD and HEALTH_RISK_THRESHOLD in kindling/config.py.
3
Replace the single shared US school calendar with per-district calendars. Different districts have different break dates — this is the main accuracy improvement available in production.
4
Replace localtunnel with a proper deployment (containerized FastAPI behind a stable URL, or a managed cloud service). This eliminates the URL-update pain on every restart.

What doesn't change

The scoring engine (features.py, pql.py, health.py), the API, the React frontend, and all 3 n8n automations stay exactly the same. The only parts that need updating are the data source, the school calendars, and the deployment URL. The architecture is correct — it just needs real data to dial in the numbers.

The design thesis

The same principle that makes Faxtract work applies here: use AI only where judgment is actually required, and keep everything else deterministic.

PQL scoring Health scoring School-day window math Prospect ranking Pipeline funnel Backtest logic Outreach logging API routing Slack channel routing

AI is reserved for the one genuinely judgment-shaped spot:

Drafting a personalized cold email from usage signals (Claude Haiku in n8n)

Writing a compelling cold email from a set of usage numbers is a judgment call — the right tone, the right opening line, what to emphasize. That's where an LLM earns its place. Everything else — the scoring, the alerts, the routing, the logging — is deterministic and free.
